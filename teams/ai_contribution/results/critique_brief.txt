You are an adversarial reviewer of an experimental design. Give the single strongest objection that could make the result of this probe misleading (in either direction), and say concretely how it would bias the verdict. Be specific and technical; <= 250 words.

Design (pre-registered, not yet run): A "headroom probe" decides whether it is worth later running an LLM-driven program search over MPS truncation schedules. Circuits: a 19-qubit heavy-hex Floquet kicked Ising circuit (RX, RZ on all qubits, RZZ(pi/3) on one of 3 edge colours per layer, 3 layers/cycle, 16 cycles), exact expectation values known (magnetisation, ZZ at graph distance 2 and 3) at cycles 4,6,8,10,13,16 = 18 instances per parameter regime. Engine: quimb CircuitMPS, swap+split for non-adjacent gates, fixed spectral qubit ordering. Cost = sum over the 48 entangling layers of sum_bonds chi_b^3 after each layer (deterministic). Metric = median over the 18 instances of log10|error|. Budget levels C_k = cost of constant bond dimension chi=8,16,32.
Baseline = best CONSTANT setting (grid: 17 chi values up to 64 x 11 singular-value cutoffs incl. discarded-weight cutoffs) with cost <= C_k. Searchers over SCHEDULES (chi(cycle) piecewise-linear in log2 chi through 4 knots, plus one discarded-weight cutoff): random search (3 seeds, same number of in-budget evaluations as the baseline grid) and a greedy coordinate hill-climb started from the best constant setting (187 evaluations). Dev regime: one parameter point; held-out: two other parameter points with a different edge colouring. Headroom declared if at >=2 of 3 levels the best dev schedule beats the best constant by >=0.10 log10 on dev AND on held-out beats the best constant setting (chosen in hindsight on the held-out regime, subject to cost <= the schedule's held-out cost) by mean >=0.05 with both regimes >0. Otherwise "no headroom" (or "dev-only").
